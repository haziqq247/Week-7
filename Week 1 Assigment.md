1.Weak passwords (Vulnerabilities)
Using simple or common passwords such as “123456” or “password” is a vulnerability. These passwords are easy for attackers to guess using password-cracking tools, which can lead to unauthorized access to systems.

2.Outdated or unpatched software (Vulnerabilities)
When software or operating systems are not updated regularly, they may contain security weaknesses. Attackers can exploit these known vulnerabilities to gain access or install malicious programs.

3.Poor access control (Vulnerabilities)
If a system allows many users to access sensitive information without proper restrictions, it becomes a vulnerability. This can allow unauthorized users to view, change, or delete important data.

4.Using unsecured public Wi-Fi (Vulnerabilities)
Connecting to public Wi-Fi networks without proper protection can expose devices to security risks. Attackers on the same network may be able to monitor or intercept the data being transmitted.

5.Lack of cybersecurity awareness among employees (Vulnerabilities)
Employees who are not trained in cybersecurity may accidentally expose sensitive information. For example, they may click suspicious links or download unsafe files.

6.Malware attacks (Threats)
Malware such as viruses, worms, or trojans can infect computer systems. These programs can damage files, steal information, or disrupt normal system operations.

7.Phishing attacks (Threats)
Phishing is when attackers send fake emails or messages pretending to be from trusted organizations. Their goal is to trick users into revealing sensitive information such as passwords or banking details.

8.Man-in-the-Middle (MITM) attacks (Threats)
In this type of attack, a hacker secretly intercepts communication between two parties. The attacker may steal data or alter the information being sent.

9.Ransomware attacks (Threats)
Ransomware is a type of malware that locks or encrypts a victim’s files. The attacker then demands payment to restore access to the data.

10.Unauthorized access by hackers (Threats)
Hackers may attempt to break into systems or networks without permission. Once inside, they can steal confidential information, modify data, or disrupt services.
